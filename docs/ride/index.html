01.3Ride readyRun before a long day

CL 01

Pre-ride check

Two minutes, cold, before the engine starts. In this order because each step tells you whether the next one matters.

  • TyresPressure by gauge, then look at the tread and the sidewall for cuts and embedded stones.
  • ChainSlack at the tightest point of rotation, not wherever it happens to be standing.
  • BrakesLever and pedal travel, then fluid level if the reservoir has a window.
  • LightsHead, brake, indicators. Brake light on both the lever and the pedal.
  • FluidsOil level on level ground, plus any leak that is new since yesterday.
  • FastenersAxle nuts, sprocket bolts, anything you or anyone else touched recently.
CL 02

Weather and terrain readiness

Judged the night before, then again at the door. A route that is fine in the dry may be a different route entirely.

  • Rain windowWhen it is expected to start, not just whether it will rain.
  • SurfaceMurram sections turn to clay quickly and stay that way after the rain stops.
  • River crossingsSeasonal crossings rise fast and fall slowly. Ask locally rather than guessing.
  • HeatMidday on the equator is a fatigue problem before it is a comfort problem.
  • LightWork backwards from sunset and add an hour of margin, not ten minutes.
  • AltitudeA cold, wet descent after a hot climb catches riders dressed for the start.
CL 05

Emergency contact preparation

Prepared before the ride, because none of it is possible to arrange afterwards.

  • Route sharedOne person who knows where you are going and roughly when to expect you.
  • Check in timeAn agreed time, and an agreed action if you miss it.
  • Local numbersStored offline in the phone and written on paper in the bike.
  • MedicalBlood group, allergies and conditions, somewhere someone will find them.
  • PowerA way to charge the phone that does not depend on the bike running.
  • Offline mapDownloaded for the whole route before you leave signal, including the return.

01.4Service intervalsTarmac against murram

Service intervals compared between sealed and unsealed use
ItemTarmac MurramWhy it differs
Chain clean and lube 800 km 300 km Dust turns lubricant into grinding paste. Clean, do not top up.
Engine oil 3000 km 2000 km Heat and low average speed both shorten this. Filter every second change at minimum.
Air filter 6000 km 1500 km The single largest difference between sealed and unsealed use.
Tyre pressure Weekly Weekly Adjust for load, not just for the sticker figure.
Brake pads Inspect 5000 km Inspect 2500 km Grit accelerates pad and disc wear together.
Spark plug 8000 km 6000 km Read it before you replace it. The old plug is a free diagnostic.
Valve clearance 12000 km 10000 km Noise is a late symptom. Measure on the interval rather than waiting to hear it.
Fork oil 20000 km 12000 km Rough surface work is what actually consumes damping performance.

01.5The first ninety secondsSEQ 01

Position and visibility come before the machine. Every time, including when the fault seems trivial.

  1. 01

    Get off the carriageway

    Push the bike fully clear of the lane if it will not run. A partly clear bike is a hazard, not a stopped vehicle.

  2. 02

    Make yourself visible

    High visibility on, hazards on if fitted, and a warning marker back up the road further than feels necessary.

  3. 03

    Stand off the road

    Not between the bike and traffic. Uphill and behind a barrier if there is one.

  4. 04

    Check yourself, then the pillion

    Adrenaline hides injury. Do this deliberately before you look at the motorcycle.

  5. 05

    Fix your position

    Note where you are precisely enough to tell someone. Nearest marker, town, junction, direction of travel.

  6. 06

    Then diagnose

    Only now. The machine has waited this long and will wait another minute.

01.6If the ride has stoppedSEQ 02

Work down this list in order. Steps one to three matter whether or not any recovery service is available to you.

  1. 01

    Safety first, always

    Get clear of moving traffic, make yourself visible, and check everyone before you check the machine. If anyone is injured, contact local emergency services immediately. Frontier Moto is not an emergency service.

  2. 02

    Fix your location

    Road name or number, direction of travel, the last town you passed and roughly how far back. A screenshot of your map position is worth more than a description.

  3. 03

    Describe the machine

    Make, model, approximate weight, whether the rear wheel turns freely, and whether it can be rolled or must be lifted.

  4. 04

    Describe the fault plainly

    What happened, at what speed, what it sounded like, and what it does now. Not what you think is broken.

  5. 05

    State your safety status

    Whether you are somewhere safe, whether you have water, whether it is getting dark, and whether you are alone.

  6. 06

    Then request

    With the first five ready, a request takes under a minute instead of a frustrating ten.
