W.1What the workshop handlesAnd what it does not

The workshop handles routine service, diagnostics and pre-route inspections. We work on the machines people actually ride here, which means commuters and working bikes as readily as touring machines.

Handled here

  • +Routine service and scheduled maintenance
  • +Pre-route and pre-purchase inspection
  • +Chain, sprocket, brake and tyre work
  • +Electrical and charging diagnostics
  • +Suspension service and setup for load
  • +Fault finding on machines that other workshops have already looked at

Referred out

  • →Engine rebuilds and machining, which we refer out
  • →Paint and bodywork
  • →Insurance accident repair

Saying this up front saves you a journey. Where we refer a job out, we will name someone who does it properly rather than leave you to find out.

W.3Preparing your motorcycleFive things, before you arrive

CL 06

Preparing a motorcycle for a workshop visit

Doing these five things before you arrive shortens the job and makes the estimate more accurate.

  • Write the symptomWhen it happens, at what speed, hot or cold, and what changes it. Not your diagnosis.
  • Bring the historyWhat was done last, by whom, and how long ago in kilometres.
  • Empty the luggageAnything you need that day, take with you. Anything you do not, leave at home.
  • FuelEnough to run and road test, not a full tank, which makes some jobs harder.
  • Say the budgetThe number you want to be called at before work continues.

Why the symptom matters more than the diagnosis

A rider who says the bike is misfiring has already narrowed the search, often in the wrong direction. A rider who says it loses power above four thousand revolutions when the engine is hot, and only in the rain, has handed the workshop most of the answer.

Write it down before you set off. By the time you arrive you will have stopped noticing the detail that matters.

W.4How the work is quoted and recordedPractical detail

  • How we quoteLabour by time, parts at cost plus a stated margin. You are called before the estimate is exceeded.
  • PartsWe will fit parts you supply, and we will tell you plainly if we think they are the wrong ones.
  • DiagnosisIf we cannot find a fault we say so and charge for the time, not for a guess fitted as a repair.
  • RecordsEvery job leaves with a written record of what was measured, not just what was replaced.
  • WaitingShort jobs can be waited for. Anything over two hours is better dropped off.
  • Test ridingWe road test after any brake, tyre, chain or suspension work, and we tell you if we did not.
